The procedure, in full.

Fig. 2 — Assembly

There are three steps. There is no step where you look up how much baking soda goes in.

1.0

Mix

Empty the dry pouch into a bowl. Add the wet pouch and 30 ml of water. Gloves on, combine by hand until no dry flour remains. It will look like dough, because it is dough.

3 min
2.0

Scoop

Six portions, roughly golf-ball plus, onto the supplied parchment. Leave two inches between them. They spread.

7 min
3.0

Bake

350°F for 12 to 15 minutes. Remove when the edges are set and the centres still look slightly underdone. They finish on the tray. Resting ten minutes is recommended and almost never observed.

15 min

A cup of flour is not a unit of mass.

Fig. 3 — Why any of this

Scoop it straight from the bag and you pack it. Spoon it in and level it off and you don't. Same cup, same flour, thirty grams apart — which across six cookies is the whole difference between chewy and dry.

Scooped from the bag
120–150 g
Spooned and levelled
120–135 g
Weighed by us
127 g, every time
110 g135 g160 g

We took the three variables that break this recipe — flour mass, butter temperature, mixing time — and moved two of them to our side of the box.

Fig. 4 — Finished specimen

Two chocolates, toffee, caramel, pretzels, oats, cherries.

Sweet, salty, chewy, crunchy, in one cookie. The add-ins are the part a box mix leaves to your cupboard and your judgement. We put them in the pouch.

Principal investigator

Our mom, Michele.

Chemical engineer by trade, baker by heart. She spent years running the same recipe until it stopped depending on who was making it — which, if you have ever handed someone a recipe and received back a different cookie, is the actual problem with baking.

We weighed it, pouched it and put it in a box. Everything she took out, you no longer have to get right.
